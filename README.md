# Component Sourcing Toolkit

Small, practical files for engineers and purchasers who source electronic components. No signup, no spreadsheet macros, no affiliate links — just templates you can copy into your own workflow.

## What is here

| File | What it is for |
|---|---|
| `bom-template.csv` | A BOM layout with the four columns a supplier needs to quote accurately. Fill this in before you ask for pricing and you avoid the usual two rounds of questions. |
| `packaging-guide.md` | Reel vs cut tape vs tray. When the cheaper cut tape is not cheaper after line downtime. |
| `date-code-guide.md` | How to read date codes and what moisture exposure actually does to parts sitting in stock. |
| `incoming-checklist.md` | What to check when a shipment arrives, and what a photo cannot tell you. |
| `common-parts.csv` | 30 real part numbers with manufacturer, category, package and a few key parameters, as a format example. |

## Why the BOM template matters

Most quote requests come back with questions because the buyer sent a list of part numbers and quantities and nothing else. A supplier cannot tell you whether 50 pieces is enough when the reel is 3000, or whether the part you want is the 2.5 V or 3.3 V version of the same family. The template asks for manufacturer, part number, quantity, and target price or target delivery date. Four columns. That is usually enough.

## About the sample data

The part numbers in `common-parts.csv` are real and the fields follow the layout used on [icdepo.com](https://icdepo.com/), a Shenzhen component sourcing desk. Check the current datasheet before designing anything in — sample listings are a format reference, not a substitute for the manufacturer's document.
