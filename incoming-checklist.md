# Incoming parts: what to check

A short list for the receiving bench. It will not catch everything, and it should not be treated as a substitute for a lab test, but it catches the common mistakes.

## Before opening

- Does the shipping label match the purchase order, part number and quantity?
- Is the moisture barrier bag intact, and what does the humidity indicator card show?
- Is there a lot number or date code on the label, and does it match the quoted stock?

## On the bench

- Marking: correct part number, correct manufacturer logo, no signs of re-marking or sanding.
- Package: correct pin count and body size, leads straight, no bent or missing pins.
- Lot consistency: if a single delivery contains several date codes, ask why. Mixed lots in one bag are a common sign of repackaging.
- Physical condition: no cracks, no corrosion on leads, no unusual weight difference between samples.

## What a photo will not tell you

A clear photo of the marking proves that the marking exists. It does not prove the die inside is correct, that the part was not relabelled, or that it survived storage. For a part that matters, electrical testing or decapsulation is the only real answer. For a part that does not matter, do not spend more on testing than the part is worth.

## If something looks wrong

Keep the parts, the bag and the label untouched, and take photos before contacting the supplier. Once the parts have been opened, soldered or mixed with other stock, a claim becomes much harder.
