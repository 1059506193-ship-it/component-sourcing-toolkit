# Reel, cut tape or tray

Packing style is one of those fields people leave blank on a purchase order, then pay for later. Three common forms:

**Full reel.** Factory packing, sealed, with a label showing quantity, lot and date code. This is what you want for production. The reel fits a feeder directly and the count is the manufacturer's count.

**Cut tape.** A length of the same tape cut from a reel, usually with a stated quantity. Fine for prototypes and rework. It does not have the original reel label, and a feeder may not accept the leading and trailing empty section unless the operator trims it.

**Tray.** Used for larger packages, BGAs and parts that do not tape well. Trays stack, but they take more space in shipping and the parts can shift if the tray is not secured.

## The cost trap

Cut tape can be a few cents cheaper per piece on paper. On an SMT line, a strip that jams or a count that is ten pieces short stops the feeder while someone rethreads it. Two hours of line time costs more than the saving on a reel of 3000. Ask for a full reel when the annual usage clearly supports it, and accept cut tape for engineering builds.

## What to put on the PO

- Packing form: full reel, cut tape, tray or loose
- Reel quantity, if a full reel
- Whether partial reels are acceptable
- Whether the original manufacturer label must be intact

If the packing form is not stated, the supplier will ship whatever is cheapest in stock that day. That is not always what your line expects.
