# Reading date codes

A date code is a manufacturer's marking for when the part was assembled or packaged. The format differs by vendor: four digits often mean week and year (2526 = week 26 of 2025), some vendors use year and week, some use a month code. Always check the vendor's own decoding before drawing conclusions.

## What an old date code does and does not mean

A 2023 part is not automatically bad. A part that has been stored dry, sealed and within its shelf life can be perfectly usable. What matters is the storage history, not the print on the bag.

The real risk is moisture. Plastic packages absorb water. When a moist part goes through reflow, the water turns to steam and can crack the package or separate the die from the lead frame. This is why moisture sensitive parts arrive in a sealed moisture barrier bag with a desiccant and a humidity indicator card.

## Practical checks

- Ask for the date code and the lot number before the order is confirmed, not after.
- For moisture sensitive parts, ask whether the moisture barrier bag is still sealed and what the humidity indicator card reads.
- If the bag has been open for longer than the floor life stated on the label, the parts need baking before reflow. Baking has a limit too; too many cycles affect solderability.
- For long storage, ask whether the stock has been retested or resealed.

Parts with old date codes are often perfectly good for repair work and low-volume production. They are a poor choice for a product that will sit in a warehouse for another three years before it is assembled.
